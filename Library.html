The Proximity–Value Decision Framework

People do not abandon high-value opportunities because they lack importance. They abandon them because emotional discomfort silently overpowers strategic judgment — and the mind reframes this avoidance as wisdom.

A decision architecture for mapping any significant relationship, collaboration, opportunity, or professional situation across two axes: strategic value and emotional discomfort. When high value and high discomfort collide, the decision enters the Pyrrhic Zone — the place where people often commit strategic self-harm while believing they are protecting themselves.
