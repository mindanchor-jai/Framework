The Proximity–Value Decision Framework

People don't abandon high-value opportunities because they lack importance. They abandon them because emotional discomfort silently overpowers strategic judgment — and the mind reframes this avoidance as wisdom.

A system for mapping every significant relationship, decision, or collaboration against two axes: how much strategic value it holds, and how much emotional discomfort it creates. When those two dimensions collide in the same situation — you're in what this framework calls the Pyrrhic Zone. That's where most self-harm happens, dressed as self-protection.
